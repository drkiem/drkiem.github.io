1. Course Introduction

CIE IGCSE Physics (Syllabus Code: 0625) provides students with a thorough and engaging foundation in the fundamental principles of the physical universe. Through a balance of rigorous theoretical exploration and practical experimental investigations, students gain deep scientific insight into motion, forces, energy, thermal effects, wave behaviors, electricity, magnetism, nuclear structure, and space physics.

Assessment Outline

The CIE IGCSE Physics 0625 qualification is assessed through three examination components. Candidates who have studied the Core curriculum are entered for Paper 1, Paper 3, and either Paper 5 or Paper 6. Candidates who have studied the Extended curriculum (Core + Supplement) are entered for Paper 2, Paper 4, and either Paper 5 or Paper 6.

Syllabus Structure

The updated Cambridge IGCSE Physics syllabus is organized into 6 major subject domains plus practical investigation skills:

NoteTopic 1: Motion, Forces and Energy

1.1: Physical quantities and measurement techniques
1.2: Motion (speed, velocity, acceleration, motion graphs)
1.3: Mass, weight and density
1.4: Effects of forces (Hooke’s law, resultant forces, circular motion)
1.5: Turning effect and moments
1.6: Momentum and impulse
1.7: Energy, work and power
1.8: Energy resources
1.9: Pressure (fluid pressure, barometers, manometers)

NoteTopic 2: Thermal Physics

2.1: Kinetic particle model of matter (states, Brownian motion, evaporation)
2.2: Thermal properties and temperature (heat capacity, expansion)
2.3: Transfer of thermal energy (conduction, convection, radiation)

NoteTopic 3: Waves

3.1: General properties of waves (transverse, longitudinal, reflection, refraction, diffraction)
3.2: Light (reflection, refraction, total internal reflection, thin lenses, dispersion)
3.3: Electromagnetic spectrum (properties, uses, hazards)
3.4: Sound (speed, echoes, ultrasound)

NoteTopic 4: Electricity and Magnetism

4.1: Simple phenomena of magnetism (magnetic fields, magnetic materials)
4.2: Electrical quantities (charge, current, electromotive force, potential difference, resistance)
4.3: Electric circuits (series/parallel, component characteristics, circuit diagrams)
4.4: Electrical safety and hazards
4.5: Electromagnetic effects (induction, AC generator, transformers, motors)

NoteTopic 5: Nuclear Physics

5.1: The nuclear model of the atom (atomic structure, isotopes, alpha scattering)
5.2: Radioactivity (alpha, beta, gamma emissions, radioactive decay, half-life, safety)

NoteTopic 6: Space Physics

6.1: Earth and the Solar System (planetary motion, gravitational field strength, orbits)
6.2: Stars and the Universe (Sun, life cycle of stars, redshift, expanding universe, cosmic microwave background)

NotePractical Assessment & Experimental Skills

P.1: Techniques, apparatus and materials
P.2: Planning experiments and investigations
P.3: Observations, measurements and estimates
P.4: Evaluating observations and experimental data
P.5: Evaluating experimental methods and suggesting improvements
