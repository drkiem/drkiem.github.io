1. Course Introduction

Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation (AI) Standard Level (SL) introduces students to the practical applications of mathematics in real-world contexts, modeling basic economic and physical systems, and foundational statistics and probability. It is designed for students interested in pursuing careers in Arts, Humanities, Social Sciences, Business, or Languages at the university level.

Assessment Outline

The IB Mathematics AI SL course is assessed through two final written examinations (80%) and an independent mathematical exploration task (20%). A graphic display calculator (GDC) is required for all written examinations:

Assessment Component Duration Calculator Policy Marks Weighting
Paper 1 (Short Questions) 90 minutes (1.5 hours) Calculator required (GDC required) 80 marks 40%
Paper 2 (Long Questions) 90 minutes (1.5 hours) Calculator required (GDC required) 80 marks 40%
Internal Assessment (IA) - Exploration Independent research (12-20 pages) No restrictions 20 marks 20%

Syllabus Structure

The syllabus is organized into five core topics at the standard level (Standard Level Core):

NoteTopic 1: Number and algebra

1.1: Operations in standard form
1.2: Arithmetic sequences and series
1.3: Geometric sequences and series
1.4: Financial applications
1.5: Laws of exponents with integer exponents
1.6: Approximation and significant figures
1.7: Amortization and annuities using technology
1.8: Systems of linear equations and polynomial solutions

NoteTopic 2: Functions

2.1: Straight lines: equations, gradient, intercepts
2.2: Concept of a function: domain, range, notation
2.3: Graphs of functions
2.4: Key features of graphs
2.5: Financial and modeling applications
2.6: Mathematical modeling process

NoteTopic 3: Geometry and trigonometry

3.1: Distance and midpoint in 3D space
3.2: Right-angled trigonometry
3.3: Sine and cosine rules, area of a triangle
3.4: Circle geometry: arc length and sector area
3.5: Equations of perpendicular bisectors
3.6: Voronoi diagrams

NoteTopic 4: Statistics and probability

4.1: Population, sampling, discrete and continuous data
4.2: Data presentation: frequency distributions
4.3: Measures of central tendency and dispersion
4.4: Bivariate data, correlation, and regression
4.5: Probability of events, Venn and tree diagrams
4.6: Independent and mutually exclusive events
4.7: Discrete random variables and expectation
4.8: Binomial distribution
4.9: Normal distribution
4.10: Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient
4.11: Chi-squared tests for independence and goodness of fit

NoteTopic 5: Calculus

5.1: Introduction to limits
5.2: Increasing and decreasing functions
5.3: Derivative of power functions and basic rules
5.4: Tangents and normals equations
5.5: Indefinite integration as anti-differentiation
5.6: Stationary points and local maximum/minimum
5.7: Optimization problems
5.8: Trapezoidal rule for approximating areas
