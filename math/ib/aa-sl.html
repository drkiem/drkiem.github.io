NoteCourse Portal: IB Mathematics AA SL (Analysis & Approaches)

Welcome, students! This specialized section contains syllabus breakdowns, core study notes, targeted practice question banks, and actual exam past papers organized dynamically to optimize your learning and help you achieve a Grade 7.

1. Course Introduction

Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (AA) Standard Level (SL) is designed for students who want to develop a strong analytical foundation in mathematics. The course focuses on theoretical mathematical concepts, algebraic analysis, functions, trigonometry, statistics, and an introduction to differential and integral calculus. It is highly suitable for students planning to study Economics, Business, Medicine, or Social Sciences at the university level.

Assessment Outline

The IB Mathematics AA SL course is assessed through two final written examinations (80%) and an independent mathematical exploration task (20%). A graphic display calculator (GDC) is required only for Paper 2:

Assessment Component Duration Calculator Policy Marks Weighting
Paper 1 (Section A & B) 90 minutes (1.5 hours) Non-calculator (Non-GDC) 80 marks 40%
Paper 2 (Section A & B) 90 minutes (1.5 hours) Calculator allowed (GDC required) 80 marks 40%
Internal Assessment (IA) - Exploration Independent research (12-20 pages) No restrictions 20 marks 20%

Syllabus Structure

The syllabus is organized into five core topics at the standard level (Standard Level Core):

NoteTopic 1: Number and algebra

1.1: Operations in standard form
1.2: Arithmetic sequences and series
1.3: Geometric sequences and series
1.4: Financial applications
1.5: Laws of exponents and logarithms
1.6: Simple deductive proofs
1.7: Rational exponents
1.8: Infinite geometric series
1.9: Binomial theorem

NoteTopic 2: Functions

2.1: Straight lines: equations, gradient, intercepts
2.2: Concept of a function: domain, range, notation
2.3: Graphs of functions
2.4: Key features of graphs
2.5: Composite and inverse functions
2.6: Quadratic functions and their graphs
2.7: Quadratic equations and discriminant
2.8: Reciprocal functions and graphs
2.9: Exponential and logarithmic functions
2.10: Solving equations graphically and analytically
2.11: Transformations of graphs

NoteTopic 3: Geometry and trigonometry

3.1: Distance and midpoint in 3D space
3.2: Right-angled trigonometry
3.3: Sine and cosine rules, area of a triangle
3.4: Radian measure, arc length, sector area
3.5: Unit circle trig definitions
3.6: Pythagorean identity and double angle formulae
3.7: Trigonometric graphs
3.8: Solving trigonometric equations

NoteTopic 4: Statistics and probability

4.1: Population, sampling, discrete and continuous data
4.2: Data presentation: frequency tables, histograms, box plots
4.3: Measures of central tendency and dispersion
4.4: Linear correlation of bivariate data
4.5: Probability of events, Venn and tree diagrams
4.6: Independent and mutually exclusive events
4.7: Discrete random variables and expectation
4.8: Binomial distribution
4.9: Normal distribution
4.10: Regression line and predictions
4.11: Conditional probability
4.12: Standardized normal variables (z-scores)

NoteTopic 5: Calculus

5.1: Concept of limits
5.2: Increasing and decreasing functions
5.3: Derivative of power functions and basic rules
5.4: Tangents and normals equations
5.5: Indefinite integration as anti-differentiation
5.6: Derivative rules: product, quotient, and chain rules
5.7: Second derivative, concavity, points of inflection
5.8: Local maximum and minimum points
5.9: Kinematic applications
5.10: Indefinite integrals of standard functions
5.11: Definite integrals and area under a curve
