1. Course Introduction

Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Chemistry (Syllabus Code: 4CH1) is designed to provide students with a detailed understanding of the fundamental principles of chemistry. Through theoretical study and practical investigations, candidates learn how substances are structured, how they react with one another, and how chemical processes are applied in industrial production and everyday life. This linear qualification prepares students for further academic steps, including Edexcel A-Level Chemistry or IB DP Chemistry.

Assessment Outline

The Pearson Edexcel International GCSE (9–1) in Chemistry (4CH1) is assessed through two externally examined papers. Both papers are taken at the end of the course. Calculators are permitted in both exams.

Assessment Paper Target Curriculum Format & Duration Marks Weighting
Paper 1C (4CH1/1C) Core Syllabus Content Written exam featuring multiple-choice, short-answer, calculations, and structured questions — 2 hours 110 marks 61.1%
Paper 2C (4CH1/2C) All Syllabus Content (Core and Bold/C material) Written exam featuring multiple-choice, short-answer, calculations, and open-response questions — 1 hour 15 minutes 70 marks 38.9%

Syllabus Structure

The Edexcel IGCSE Chemistry syllabus is divided into 4 main topic areas, encompassing core concepts, laboratory investigations, and industrial processes:

NoteTopic 1: Principles of Chemistry

1a: States of matter
1b: Elements, compounds and mixtures
1c: Atomic structure
1d: The Periodic Table
1e: Chemical formulae, equations and calculations (Stoichiometry)
1f: Ionic bonding
1g: Covalent bonding
1h: Metallic bonding
1i: Electrolysis

NoteTopic 2: Inorganic Chemistry

2a: Group 1 (alkali metals) — lithium, sodium and potassium
2b: Group 7 (halogens) — chlorine, bromine and iodine
2c: Gases in the atmosphere
2d: Reactivity series
2e: Extraction and uses of metals
2f: Acids, alkalis and titrations
2g: Acids, bases and salt preparations
2h: Chemical tests for ions and gases

NoteTopic 3: Physical Chemistry

3a: Energetics (temperature changes, bond energies)
3b: Rates of reaction (collision theory, catalysts)
3c: Reversible reactions and equilibria (dynamic equilibrium, Le Chatelier’s principle)

NoteTopic 4: Organic Chemistry

4a: Introduction to organic chemistry (naming, functional groups)
4b: Crude oil (fractional distillation, cracking)
4c: Alkanes (substitution reactions)
4d: Alkenes (addition reactions)
4e: Alcohols (fermentation, oxidation)
4f: Carboxylic acids (reactions with metals and carbonates)
4g: Esters (esterification, ethyl ethanoate)
4h: Synthetic polymers (addition and condensation polymers)
